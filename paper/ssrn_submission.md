**Title**
Chinese Financial Writing in the Age of Generative AI: Linguistic Change, Model Fingerprints, and the Limits of Detection

**Author**
Andy Yu — Independent Researcher — yhhzju@gmail.com

**Abstract**（SSRN 上限 2,500 字符，下面约 1,450 字符）

We port the excess-vocabulary method developed for English academic abstracts to Chinese sell-side analyst reports (Sina Finance report summaries, 2019–2026; 94,201 reports in matched calendar windows and a 95-month panel of 59,070 reports). The mean document frequency of 151 manually classified style words rises from 0.113 in 2019 to 0.174 in 2026, while words describing economic conditions show no trend. The rise is flat in 2023–2024 after ChatGPT and accelerates from 2025, with a break near April 2025 after the release of DeepSeek-R1; about a third of the 2019–2026 rise predates LLMs. In paired polishing, rewriting and generation experiments on 2019 reports, DeepSeek barely moves the style-word group (1.01–1.13x) whereas Qwen raises it 1.64–2.53x, so whether the word list is a model fingerprint depends on the model. Off-the-shelf Chinese AI-text detectors have no discriminative power on this genre, and a purpose-trained detector is highly sensitive to its negative-sample design. We find no stable relation between style-word density and post-report abnormal returns, abnormal volume or forecast error. Neither word frequencies nor detectors can reliably estimate how much analyst-report text AI produced; the markers move with each model generation.
